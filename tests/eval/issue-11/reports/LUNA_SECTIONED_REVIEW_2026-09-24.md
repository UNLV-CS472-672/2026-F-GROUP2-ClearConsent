# Manual spot-check of Luna's 229 full-policy findings

**Verdict:** Luna mostly extracted real, source-supported policy statements in this review, but the 229 records are **not 229 clean, distinct takeaways**. The core summaries in the 30-record sample tracked the policy text. Some `recipients` fields confused an advertiser, collaborator, app, or the policy owner with a recipient of personal data; one qualification cited a blank line; and the Facebook legal-basis appendix produced overlapping findings that would need consolidation for a user-facing summary. This is an exploratory editorial spot-check by one non-legal reviewer, not a precision or recall score, legal review, or comparison with Terra.

## What was checked

- Read-only review of the saved [bounded-section run](LUNA_SECTIONED_FULL_RUN_2026-09-24.md) and the exact [pinned policy inputs](../fixtures/full-policy-cases.json). No additional model calls or token cost.
- All **229** records: the existing validators found 15/15 completed, schema-valid section responses and one unresolved citation, `L0895`, a blank Facebook source line. Exact practice-plus-citation duplicate check found zero, but that does **not** detect semantic overlap.
- A reproducible sample of **30 records**, two per completed call, selected by SHA-256 of case ID, section index, and finding index before judging content. Run `node tests/eval/issue-11/scripts/print-sectioned-review-sample.mjs <Spotify-and-Facebook-log> <Facebook-only-log>` to print the same sample from the local Git-ignored result logs. Every sampled summary, listed data category, purpose, recipient, condition, and citation was compared with its cited source lines. The six known-claim anchors in the [run report](LUNA_SECTIONED_FULL_RUN_2026-09-24.md) are a separate, targeted check.

The categories below are mutually exclusive editorial verdicts for this sample, not statistical estimates for all 229:

| Verdict                                     | Count | Meaning                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Usable as written                           |    13 | Relevant claim and material fields supported by the cited text.                                                       |
| Supported, needs consolidation or splitting |     9 | Real practice, but broad or overlapping with another finding; preserve scope and legal-basis conditions when merging. |
| Recipient-field problem                     |     5 | Core practice is supported, but the `recipients` entry is unsupported or unhelpfully names the first-party company.   |
| Citation/qualification error                |     1 | Core claim is supported, but one qualification cites blank `L0895` and its linked controls are not precisely cited.   |
| Peripheral to personal-data practices       |     2 | Supported policy statement, but weak material for a privacy-practice summary.                                         |

**Important distinction:** I did not find a clearly invented _core summary_ in this sample. That does not mean the full structured records are all correct: at least three sampled recipient entries are not supported as data disclosures, and two more name the first-party company as its own recipient. The sample is small and was reviewed by one reader, so it cannot establish the issue's unsupported-claim percentage or full-document recall. The [official OpenAI evaluation guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) likewise calls for task-specific criteria and human review alongside automated checks.

## Practical reading of the result

Luna appears capable of finding and describing many real privacy-policy practices at low cost. The biggest remaining issue in this test is **output quality control**, not whether it can find anything: the current schema allows “recipient” to be filled when the policy merely names an advertiser, research collaborator, platform, or the controller itself, and the long Facebook legal-basis appendix yields overlapping records. A line-ID validator cannot catch those interpretation errors. Before calling the 229 findings product-ready or using them to recommend Luna over Terra, we need a reviewed definition of what counts as a recipient and a rule for presenting repeated practices with their distinct conditions. Do not use this sample's bucket counts as a precision or recall estimate.
